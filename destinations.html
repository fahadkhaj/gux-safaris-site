Every Corner of Tanzania, In One Place

From the Serengeti's endless plains to Zanzibar's reef-lined beaches — here's the full GUX Safaris map, organized so you can actually plan a trip around it.

Northern Circuit Southern & Western Safari Parks Zanzibar & the Spice Islands Trekking & Adventure

Northern Circuit

Tanzania's classic safari route — big skies, big cats, and the world's most famous crater.

Arusha National Park

A compact park in GUX Safaris' backyard — Mount Meru, the Momella Lakes, giraffe and colobus monkeys, and an easy half-day safari from town.

Explore Arusha

Mount Meru

Tanzania's second-highest peak and Kilimanjaro's quieter, steeper cousin — a 3-4 day climb through forest, moorland, and a dramatic ash cone with far fewer trekkers.

Ask us about this

Mount Kilimanjaro

Africa's rooftop. Six routes, five climate zones, one unforgettable sunrise at Uhuru Peak. We run Machame, Marangu, Lemosho and more.

Explore Mount

Serengeti National Park

Endless plains, the Great Migration, and the highest big-cat density in Africa. The park every safari list starts with.

Explore Serengeti

Ngorongoro Crater

A collapsed volcanic caldera packed with wildlife in a single, walled-in ecosystem — one of the most reliable places on Earth to see the Big Five in a day.

Explore Ngorongoro

Tarangire National Park

Baobab-studded plains and Tanzania's biggest elephant herds, especially in the dry season when the Tarangire River is the only water around.

Explore Tarangire

Lake Manyara

Tree-climbing lions, flamingo-pink shallows, and dense groundwater forest packed into a small, scenic park below the Rift Valley escarpment.

Explore Lake

Lake Natron

A blood-red soda lake beneath the active Ol Doinyo Lengai volcano — flamingo breeding grounds and some of Tanzania's starkest, most alien scenery.

Explore Lake

Mkomazi National Park

An underrated dry-country park bordering Kenya's Tsavo, home to black rhino and wild dog sanctuaries and wide-open acacia savanna.

Ask us about this

Southern & Western Safari Parks

Wilder, quieter parks for travelers who want Tanzania without the crowds.

Nyerere National Park (Selous)

Africa's largest game reserve turned national park — boat safaris on the Rufiji River alongside game drives and walking safaris, with a fraction of the northern circuit's crowds.

Explore Nyerere

Ruaha National Park

Tanzania's largest national park and one of Africa's great wild-dog and lion strongholds, where the Great Ruaha River draws game through the dry season.

Explore Ruaha

Mikumi National Park

The closest major park to Dar es Salaam — open floodplains often compared to the Serengeti, easily paired with Udzungwa or Ruaha.

Explore Mikumi

Saadani National Park

Tanzania's only park with an Indian Ocean beachfront — genuinely rare, walk off a game drive straight onto the sand.

Ask us about this

Udzungwa Mountains National Park

Rainforest, waterfalls, and primates found nowhere else on Earth — Tanzania's answer to a tropical hiking park, best explored on foot.

Explore Udzungwa

Gombe Stream National Park

Jane Goodall's original research site on Lake Tanganyika — chimpanzee trekking through steep forest, reached only by boat.

Explore Gombe

Mahale Mountains National Park

Remote, boat-access-only chimpanzee trekking against a backdrop of white-sand beaches on Lake Tanganyika — one of Africa's most beautiful and hardest-to-reach parks.

Ask us about this

Katavi National Park

Tanzania's wildest and least-visited major park — dry-season hippo and crocodile congregations that rival anywhere in Africa, and almost no other vehicles.

Ask us about this

Rubondo Island National Park

A forested island in Lake Victoria built around walking safaris, birding, and a pioneering chimpanzee reintroduction project.

Ask us about this

Saanane Island National Park

Tanzania's smallest national park, a short boat ride from Mwanza — an easy half-day of walking safari and lake views.

Ask us about this

Kitulo National Park

The 'Serengeti of Flowers' — a high-altitude plateau bursting into a carpet of wild orchids and rare blooms every rainy season.

Explore Kitulo

Zanzibar & the Spice Islands

White sand, turquoise water, centuries of Swahili and spice-trade history.

Stone Town

A UNESCO World Heritage old town of coral-stone buildings, carved doors, and spice-scented alleys — Zanzibar's cultural heart.

Ask us about this

Nungwi

Zanzibar's liveliest beach town on the northern tip — powder-white sand, sunset dhow cruises, and the island's best swimming beaches.

Ask us about this

Kendwa

Just south of Nungwi, with no tidal drop-off — swimmable at any hour, and home to Zanzibar's famous full-moon beach parties.

Ask us about this

Paje

The east coast's kitesurfing capital — steady trade winds, shallow turquoise lagoons, and a laid-back beach-town pace.

Ask us about this

Jambiani

A quieter stretch of east-coast beach village where seaweed farmers still work the tidal flats at low tide.

Ask us about this

Matemwe

A relaxed northeast beach with some of the island's best reef diving and snorkeling just offshore, near Mnemba Atoll.

Ask us about this

Kiwengwa

Long stretches of white sand on the northeast coast, home to some of Zanzibar's larger resorts and calm swimming water.

Ask us about this

Pongwe

A small, sheltered cove on the east coast — calm, shallow, and one of the island's most photogenic swimming bays.

Ask us about this

Kizimkazi

A south-coast fishing village and the departure point for dolphin-watching and snorkeling trips.

Ask us about this

Mnemba Island / Atoll

A protected coral atoll off the northeast coast — some of the best snorkeling and diving in East Africa.

Ask us about this

Chumbe Island

A private coral park and one of Africa's oldest marine protected areas, plus a rare coastal forest and giant coconut crabs.

Ask us about this

Prison Island (Changuu)

A short boat ride from Stone Town, home to giant Aldabra tortoises and a historic former quarantine station.

Ask us about this

Spice Farms

Guided walks through working plantations of clove, nutmeg, cinnamon, and vanilla — the trade that gave Zanzibar its name.

Ask us about this

Forodhani Gardens

Stone Town's waterfront night market — Zanzibari street food, grilled seafood, and sugarcane juice as the sun goes down.

Ask us about this

Pemba Island

Zanzibar's lush, hilly, far-less-visited sister island — dense clove forests and some of the region's most pristine diving.

Ask us about this

Trekking & Adventure

For the ones who'd rather earn the view — mountains, waterfalls, and life on foot or water.

Kilimanjaro Trekking Routes

Machame, Marangu, Lemosho, Rongai, and Umbwe — we'll help you pick the route that matches your timeline, budget, and acclimatization needs.

Explore Kilimanjaro

Mount Meru Trek

A 3-4 day climb often used as Kilimanjaro acclimatization — forest, buffalo and giraffe on the lower slopes, then a knife-edge summit ridge.

Ask us about this

Usambara Mountains

Cool, misty highlands near Lushoto with village-to-village hiking, waterfalls, and some of Tanzania's best community-based tourism.

Ask us about this

Ol Doinyo Lengai

An active volcano sacred to the Maasai — a tough overnight climb rewarded with a crater rim view over Lake Natron at sunrise.

Ask us about this

Waterfall Hikes

Sanje Falls in Udzungwa and Materuni Falls near Kilimanjaro are the two we send hikers to most — both pair well with a coffee-farm visit.

Explore Waterfall

Walking Safaris

Guided, armed walking safaris in Ruaha, Nyerere, and Rubondo — the slowest, closest way to read tracks and understand the bush.

Explore Walking

Chimpanzee Trekking

Follow habituated chimp communities through steep forest in Gombe or remote Mahale — book well ahead, permits are limited daily.

Explore Chimpanzee

Canoeing & River Safaris

Paddle the Rufiji in Nyerere or drift the Great Ruaha — a quiet, low-impact way to get close to hippos, crocs, and waterbirds.

Explore Canoeing

Diving & Snorkeling

Mnemba Atoll, Pemba's reef walls, and Chumbe's marine park cover everything from easy snorkeling to serious wreck and drift diving.

Ask us about this
